# B1 literature positioning: finite-budget best-arm identification and active evaluation (2026-11-05)

**Status:** source-verified literature reconnaissance; **no new experiments or claims of novelty**. This supplements, and does not modify, the frozen B1 v1 four-policy randomized/bootstrapped evidence. Sources accessed from established proceedings and journal pages on 2026-11-05.

## The exact B1 question, with literature vocabulary

B1 chooses **one** algorithm \(a\) from seven, after adaptively revealing at most \(B\) unique entries \(Y_{a,c}\) of a fixed 7×12 loss matrix. It seeks to minimize **terminal simple regret** on the mean over twelve cells, not cumulative reward during selection. Budget \(B\) is in atomic algorithm×cell evaluations, not graph trajectories or hours. The first seven actions observe every algorithm on a single common starting cell; only B−7 actions remain discretionary. There is **no repeated noisy observation of an identical algorithm×cell action**. The historical replay is one fixed finite instance and uses an additive Gaussian surrogate with heuristic priors.

This relates most directly to **fixed-budget pure exploration / best-arm identification (BAI)**, **ranking-and-selection / optimal computing budget allocation (OCBA)**, **Bayesian value-of-information (knowledge gradient)**, **structured/transductive experimental design**, and **active model selection**. Importantly, classical BAI/OCBA theory usually assumes noisy, *repeatable* arm pulls, and fixed-confidence methods have a different resource objective. Theoretical guarantees do not automatically apply to B1's finite, structured, without-replacement design.

## Where "optimal" is and is not justified

1. An exact Bayesian optimum at budget B is the finite-horizon policy minimizing **posterior expected terminal simple regret** with B−t observations remaining. It can be described by Bellman dynamic programming when a coherent prior and observation model are specified, but in B1's 84-action space exact computation is a large *belief-state planning* problem. The archive's complete future values may be used **only to score decisions**, never as foresight by the policy.
2. **Knowledge gradient** is optimal **for one remaining decision under its stated model** and in special other settings, but is **not** a universal finite-horizon optimal strategy.
3. **Russo's top-two optimality** concerns a particular asymptotic rate (posterior concentration/error exponent) under specified stochastic models, **not** B1's finite-horizon no-replacement simple-regret minimization.
4. **OD-LinBAI** provides an optimality bound **under a linear-bandit model** and a probability-of-misidentification criterion; this cannot be assigned to an informal adaptation on the B1 matrix.
5. The fixed-budget and fixed-confidence goals differ. B1 must use fixed-budget approaches and report **simple regret** as the primary endpoint. Best-selection frequency is a useful secondary endpoint, but algorithms that optimize it need not rank best on simple regret.

## Implications for B1 v1

- The 27.9% development reduction vs Balanced is a **valid archive-conditional finding**, but it does **not** establish an original allocation principle. Top-two targeting, elimination and optimal information design are longstanding topics.
- **B1's "budget-aware Top2" is not TTTS**. Its 50/50 coverage-to-variance-reduction schedule was constructed for this archive; the percentage was fixed before the five-budget comparison but after earlier development on the same archive. It may be useful as a practical heuristic but cannot claim theoretical optimality.
- The current **Informed** policy is **not** a canonical knowledge-gradient, UGapE or BayesGap method: it weights per-algorithm posterior variance reduction across all competing algorithms, rather than computing expected change in the final recommendation's loss. Its failure to improve aggregate regret over Balanced does **not** imply standard Bayesian best-arm identification is ineffective.
- A natural **first literature-grounded competitor** is **one-step knowledge gradient with the same B1 Gaussian model and no-replacement action set**, because it targets expected improvement in *terminal decision value*. A **second** is **gap/confidence-based BayesGap or UGapE adapted to the seven aggregate objectives**; a **third** is a carefully defined fixed-budget elimination baseline or transductive linear design. Keep the four original policies and the 4,800-episode benchmark unchanged.
- Before constructing a new algorithm, **audit assumptions**: B1's `NOISE=0.055` is a heuristic regression residual, while each historical cell-MAE is revealed as a fixed deterministic archive value. Bayesian KG calculations would be *model-conditional planning*, not a proof that errors truly follow the Gaussian prior.
- A new comparison run on the **same previously inspected archive** would be **supplementary development evidence only**. The specific benchmark definitions, priors and adaptation to nonrepeatability must be specified **before** looking at its comparison results. Do not tune the new methods to exceed 27.9%.
